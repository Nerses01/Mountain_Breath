---
title: The first frames of the year
date: 2026-06-20
teaser: Cherry blossom honey, nine days of rain, and why the first harvest is always the smallest.
---

The first harvest is always the smallest, and always the one the family
argues about most. Grandfather's rule was simple: nothing leaves the hive
before the summer solstice. We broke it by two days this year, and he
would have allowed it — nine days of rain in May pushed the cherry and
apple bloom late, and the bees made up for it in one furious June week.

Forty frames, spun cold on the 18th, jarred on the 19th. The honey is
pale, almost green in the jar, and tastes of the orchard rows below the
meadow. Hives 12 through 31 gave the best of it; if your jar carries one
of those numbers, you have the spring itself.

The royal jelly season starts next week. The cold boxes are ready.
