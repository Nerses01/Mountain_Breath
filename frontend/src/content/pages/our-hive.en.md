# Our hive

My grandfather kept nine hives on this meadow. We keep two hundred and ten.

The meadow sits at 1,400 metres, above the villages and below the ridge,
where the wildflowers follow one another from late April to the first cold
nights of October. The bees work it in the order the mountain decides:
cherry and apple blossom first, then thyme, clover and vetch, then the long
linden weeks, and last the pale autumn flowers that make the darkest honey.

## Three generations, one method

We take only the surplus comb. We spin it cold, never above the temperature
of the hive itself, and we jar it the same week. Nothing is blended across
seasons, nothing is filtered beyond a coarse sieve that lets the pollen
through, and nothing is added — not to the honey, not to the beeswax, not
to anything else on this shelf.

Every jar carries its hive number, because every hive is a slightly
different neighbourhood of the same meadow. If you find a jar you love,
tell us the number — next season we will put a jar from the same row aside
for you.

## Come and see it

If you come up the valley in July, we will put you in a veil and let you
lift a frame yourself. Write to us first — the road needs describing, and
the bees prefer visitors on sunny mornings.
